# Glossary

This table collects the core terms of this site's four blocks — phonetics, vocabulary, grammar, and exam preparation — and gives each a definition, an authoritative source, and the related page.

## Phonetics and orthography

### Phoneme
The smallest unit of sound that distinguishes meaning; when two words differ by one phoneme, those two phonemes are distinct.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Phoneme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoneme), [Wikipedia · English phonology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_phonology)
**Related pages**: [pronunciation](/pronunciation), [topic/pronunciation hard spots](/topic/pronunciation-hard-parts)

### Vowel
The vowel phoneme whose airflow passes through the mouth unobstructed; the contrast between short and long vowels is a phonemic contrast.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Vowel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vowel), [Wikipedia · IPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Phonetic_Alphabet)
**Related pages**: [pronunciation](/pronunciation), [topic/pronunciation hard spots](/topic/pronunciation-hard-parts)

### Consonant
The phoneme whose airflow is obstructed; tr, dr, ts, dz are strictly consonant clusters, but the 48-phoneme teaching convention used in China lists them as consonants.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Consonant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consonant), [Wikipedia · English phonology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_phonology)
**Related pages**: [pronunciation](/pronunciation), [topic/pronunciation hard spots](/topic/pronunciation-hard-parts)

### Minimal pair
Two words differing by one phoneme; the basic material for training listening discrimination and matching articulation.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Minimal pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_pair)
**Related pages**: [pronunciation](/pronunciation), [topic/pronunciation hard spots](/topic/pronunciation-hard-parts)

### International Phonetic Alphabet
The phonemic notation system published by the International Phonetic Association; it differs from the 48-phoneme teaching convention, so mind the difference when comparing.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · IPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Phonetic_Alphabet)
**Related pages**: [pronunciation](/pronunciation), [topic/pronunciation hard spots](/topic/pronunciation-hard-parts)

### English orthography
The spelling conventions of English; the root of the spelling-pronunciation gap is that the Great Vowel Shift changed the sounds while printing froze the old spellings.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · English orthography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_orthography), [Wikipedia · Great Vowel Shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Vowel_Shift)
**Related pages**: [history](/history), [pronunciation](/pronunciation)

### Great Vowel Shift
The systematic raising and diphthongization of vowels between about 1400 and 1700, the historical root of the English spelling-pronunciation gap.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Great Vowel Shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Vowel_Shift)
**Related pages**: [history](/history), [pronunciation](/pronunciation)

### Stress
English is stress-timed: content words stay loud, function words become weak; stress position can change part of speech and meaning.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_(linguistics)), [Wikipedia · Intonation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intonation)
**Related pages**: [pronunciation](/pronunciation), [topic/pronunciation hard spots](/topic/pronunciation-hard-parts)

### Connected speech
The linking, loss of plosion, and assimilation applied to neighboring word sounds in natural speech flow; the key to understanding spoken English.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Connected speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_speech), [Wikipedia · Linking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linking)
**Related pages**: [pronunciation](/pronunciation), [topic/pronunciation hard spots](/topic/pronunciation-hard-parts)

### Received Pronunciation
The traditional standard British pronunciation, the British phonemic basis of the 48-phoneme chart used in China.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Received Pronunciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Received_Pronunciation)
**Related pages**: [pronunciation](/pronunciation)

### American and British English spelling
The color/colour and center/centre families of differences come from deliberate rule-making in the 19th century, not by chance.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Spelling differences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_and_British_English_spelling_differences)
**Related pages**: [history](/history), [exam/high-frequency vocabulary](/exam/vocabulary)

## Vocabulary and etymology

### Etymology
The source and historical evolution of a word; etymology is a memory hook, and meaning is governed by the modern common sense.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology), [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/)
**Related pages**: [roots](/roots), [topic/root families](/topic/root-families)

### Morphology
The branch studying the structure of words (roots, affixes, inflection); new words of the coinage era are mostly rearrangements of native parts.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Morphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphology_(linguistics))
**Related pages**: [roots](/roots), [topic/root families](/topic/root-families)

### False cognate
A word from another language or word family that looks alike but means differently; a frequent blank in discrimination questions.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · False cognate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_cognate), [Wikipedia · Synonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synonym)
**Related pages**: [topic/confusing words](/topic/confusing-words), [exam/high-frequency vocabulary](/exam/vocabulary)

### Synonym
Words of the same or a similar meaning; when distinguishing, look at register, collocation, and emotional coloring.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Synonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synonym), [Wikipedia · Polysemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polysemy)
**Related pages**: [topic/confusing words](/topic/confusing-words)

### Polysemy
One word form carrying several related senses, the normal result of meaning sliding away from its origin.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Polysemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polysemy), [Wikipedia · Etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology)
**Related pages**: [topic/confusing words](/topic/confusing-words), [roots](/roots)

### Collocation
The habitual fixed or high-frequency co-occurrence of words; the core source of native feel.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Collocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collocation)
**Related pages**: [topic/confusing words](/topic/confusing-words), [exam/high-frequency oral communication](/exam/oral-communication)

### Idiom
A fixed expression whose overall meaning cannot be inferred from its literal words; the Bible and literary allusions are a major source of English idioms.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Idiom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idiom), [Wikipedia · Etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology)
**Related pages**: [history](/history), [exam/high-frequency oral communication](/exam/oral-communication)

### Vocabulary acquisition
The research framework on second-language learning patterns, the design basis for vocabulary and pronunciation practice.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Second-language acquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-language_acquisition), [Wikipedia · Language acquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_acquisition)
**Related pages**: [knowledge](/knowledge), [exam/vocabulary memorization](/exam/vocabulary-memorization)

### Academic Word List
A high-frequency word list of academic texts, the source of the backbone vocabulary list for exam preparation.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Academic Word List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_word_list), [Wikipedia · Vocabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocabulary)
**Related pages**: [exam/high-frequency vocabulary](/exam/vocabulary), [exam/vocabulary memorization](/exam/vocabulary-memorization)

## Grammar and syntax

### Grammatical tense
The combination of time (past/present/future) with aspect (simple/progressive/perfect); English has no future-tense form and relies on lexical means.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Grammatical tense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammatical_tense)
**Related pages**: [tense](/tense), [topic/tense comparison](/topic/tense-comparison)

### Subjunctive mood
The verb form expressing hypothesis, suggestion, or wish; the key point is shifting the tense one step back.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Subjunctive mood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subjunctive_mood)
**Related pages**: [topic/tense comparison](/topic/tense-comparison), [grammar](/grammar)

### Participle
The present participle marks active and progressive, the past participle passive and perfect; the logical subject must match the subject of the main clause.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Participle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Participle), [Wikipedia · Gerund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerund)
**Related pages**: [topic/non-finite verbs](/topic/non-finite-verbs), [topic/long and complex sentences](/topic/long-sentences)

### Infinitive
to + bare verb, marking purpose and futurity; one of the three non-finite forms.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Infinitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitive), [Wikipedia · Verb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verb)
**Related pages**: [topic/non-finite verbs](/topic/non-finite-verbs), [grammar](/grammar)

### Clause
Three kinds by role — nominal, attributive, adverbial; picking the connector means seeing what component the clause lacks.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clause), [Wikipedia · English clause syntax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_clause_syntax)
**Related pages**: [topic/clause connectors](/topic/clause-connectors), [topic/long and complex sentences](/topic/long-sentences)

### Relative clause
A modifying clause introduced by a relative word; a non-restrictive clause does not use that.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Relative clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_clause), [Wikipedia · Conjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjunction)
**Related pages**: [topic/clause connectors](/topic/clause-connectors), [grammar](/grammar)

### Conditional sentence
The conditional pattern; time and condition clauses use the simple present in place of the future.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Conditional sentence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_sentence), [Wikipedia · Imperative mood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperative_mood)
**Related pages**: [topic/condition and concession](/topic/condition-and-concession), [topic/tense comparison](/topic/tense-comparison)

### Passive voice
A set of be-forms in various tenses plus a past participle; intransitive verbs have no passive, and get + done is a colloquial variant.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · English passive voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_passive_voice), [Wikipedia · Passive voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_voice)
**Related pages**: [topic/passive voice](/topic/passive-voice), [exam/high-frequency grammar and translation](/exam/grammar-and-translation)

### Comparative
The comparative structures of adjectives and adverbs; much, not very, modifies a comparative.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Comparative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative), [Wikipedia · English adjectives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_adjectives)
**Related pages**: [topic/comparison structures](/topic/comparison-structures), [grammar](/grammar)

### English modal verb
The verbs expressing deduction, possibility, and obligation; modal deduction has a certainty gradient (must > may > might).
**Source**: [Wikipedia · English modal verb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_modal_verb), [Wikipedia · Auxiliary verb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary verb)
**Related pages**: [grammar](/grammar), [topic/tense comparison](/topic/tense-comparison)

### Determiner
The limiter placed before a noun phrase, including articles; a bare countable singular needs a determiner.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Determiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Determiner), [Wikipedia · English articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_articles)
**Related pages**: [grammar](/grammar), [exam/high-frequency grammar and translation](/exam/grammar-and-translation)

### Phrasal verb
A fixed structure built from a verb plus a preposition or adverb; the preposition may not be dropped when it is passivized.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Phrasal verb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrasal_verb), [Wikipedia · English prepositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_prepositions)
**Related pages**: [topic/prepositions](/topic/prepositions), [grammar](/grammar)

## Proficiency and testing

### Equivalency master's degree
The qualification route by which a person with equivalent academic standing applies for a master's degree; its national English exam is the original exam behind this site's past-paper system.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Equivalency master's degree](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%90%8C%E7%AD%89%E5%AD%A6%E5%8A%9B%E7%94%B3%E7%A1%95), [Wikipedia · National master's admission exam](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%85%A8%E5%9B%BD%E7%A0%94%E7%A9%B6%E7%94%9F%E6%8B%9B%E7%94%9F%E8%80%83%E8%AF%95)
**Related pages**: [exam/past-paper sources](/exam/papers/sources), [exam/high-frequency key points](/exam/key-points)

### TOEFL
An English proficiency test whose exam structure can serve as a reference frame for this site's exam-preparation conventions.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · TOEFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TOEFL), [Wikipedia · IELTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IELTS)
**Related pages**: [exam/mock exam guide](/exam/mock-exam-guide), [exam/2026 key-point prediction](/exam/2026-prediction)

### IELTS
An English proficiency test; together with TOEFL it is the reference frame for standardized English ability.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · IELTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IELTS), [Wikipedia · TOEFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TOEFL)
**Related pages**: [exam/mock exam guide](/exam/mock-exam-guide), [exam/exam timing](/exam/timing)

### Cloze test
A question type that leaves blanks in a text to be filled in, testing the joint ability of vocabulary and contextual inference.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Cloze test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloze_test)
**Related pages**: [exam/high-frequency cloze and completion](/exam/cloze-and-completion), [exam/high-frequency vocabulary](/exam/vocabulary)

### Reading comprehension
The ability to grasp the main idea, details, and inferences of a reading passage, including the strategy for breaking down long and complex sentences.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Reading comprehension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading_comprehension)
**Related pages**: [exam/high-frequency reading](/exam/reading), [topic/long and complex sentences](/topic/long-sentences)

### Translation
The transfer of meaning across languages; written translation requires structural restoration and terminological consistency.
**Source**: [Wikipedia · Translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation)
**Related pages**: [exam/high-frequency grammar and translation](/exam/grammar-and-translation), [exam/writing memorization](/exam/writing-memorization)

### Purdue OWL academic writing guide
Purdue's online guide to academic writing, the authoritative reference for writing conventions.
**Source**: [Purdue OWL](https://owl.purdue.edu/), [Wikipedia · Grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammar)
**Related pages**: [exam/high-frequency writing](/exam/writing), [exam/writing memorization](/exam/writing-memorization)
