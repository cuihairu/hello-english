# Confusing words

Vocabulary questions set blanks in three places: look-alike, sound-alike, and meaning-alike. Here are 14 frequently tested groups of confusable words, each with its core distinction and tappable example words. For a fuller synonym-replacement table, go to [High-frequency vocabulary](/exam/vocabulary); for the origins of word building, go to [Root families](/topic/root-families).

## Look-alikes that trick the eye

| Group | Tap to hear | Distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| affect / effect | <SpeakButton word="affect" text="affect" /> <SpeakButton word="effect" text="effect" /> | affect is the verb "to influence"; effect is usually the noun "a result" (have an effect on). effect as a verb means "to bring about, to realize" (effect change) — tested rarely |
| adapt / adopt / adept | <SpeakButton word="adapt" text="adapt" /> <SpeakButton word="adopt" text="adopt" /> <SpeakButton word="adept" text="adept" /> | adapt "to make fit", adapt to; adopt "to take up; to adopt a child"; adept is the adjective "skilled", be adept at |
| principal / principle | <SpeakButton word="principal" text="principal" /> <SpeakButton word="principle" text="principle" /> | principal "main; the headmaster"; principle "a rule", in principle "as a rule". Memory aid: your principal is your pal (companion) |
| complement / compliment | <SpeakButton word="complement" text="complement" /> <SpeakButton word="compliment" text="compliment" /> | complement "to complete", related to complete; compliment "to praise, to flatter" — the middle i evokes "I like" that kind of sentence |
| stationary / stationery | <SpeakButton word="stationary" text="stationary" /> <SpeakButton word="stationery" text="stationery" /> | stationary "at rest"; stationery "stationery goods". Both come from the same "fixed" root: a stationer was a dealer who kept a fixed stall — one word left behind the occupation, the other "not moving" |
| desert / dessert | <SpeakButton word="desert" text="desert" /> <SpeakButton word="dessert" text="dessert" /> | desert "a desert", as a verb "to desert"; dessert "an aftersweet". Double s is the sweet — tasty enough that you want two servings |

## Near-synonyms that get swapped

| Group | Tap to hear | Distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| assure / ensure / insure | <SpeakButton word="assure" text="assure" /> <SpeakButton word="ensure" text="ensure" /> <SpeakButton word="insure" text="insure" /> | assure is toward a person, the object is a person: assure sb. that…; ensure makes sure a thing happens, the object is a thing; insure refers specifically to taking out insurance |
| rise / raise / arise / arouse | <SpeakButton word="rise" text="rise" /> <SpeakButton word="raise" text="raise" /> <SpeakButton word="arise" text="arise" /> <SpeakButton word="arouse" text="arouse" /> | rise = to go up, intransitive (rise-rose-risen); raise = to lift, transitive; arise = to occur, to come up, the subject is usually problem or difficulty; arouse = to evoke, the object is usually interest or suspicion |
| historic / historical | <SpeakButton word="historic" text="historic" /> <SpeakButton word="historical" text="historical" /> | historic = having historical importance (a historic moment); historical = relating to history (historical records) |
| economic / economical | <SpeakButton word="economic" text="economic" /> <SpeakButton word="economical" text="economical" /> | economic = of the economy (economic growth); economical = thrifty, good value (an economical car) |
| sensible / sensitive | <SpeakButton word="sensible" text="sensible" /> <SpeakButton word="sensitive" text="sensitive" /> | sensible = sensible (a sensible choice); sensitive = sensitive, be sensitive to |
| considerable / considerate | <SpeakButton word="considerable" text="considerable" /> <SpeakButton word="considerate" text="considerate" /> | considerable = quite large (a considerable amount); considerate = thoughtful of others |
| respectable / respectful / respective | <SpeakButton word="respectable" text="respectable" /> <SpeakButton word="respectful" text="respectful" /> <SpeakButton word="respective" text="respective" /> | respectable = worthy of respect; respectful = showing respect; respective = each their own (back to their respective rooms) |

## lie and lay: one pair of verbs, three meanings

| Word | Changes | Meaning | Tap to hear |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| lie | lie – lay – lain | to lie down, intransitive | <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="I lay on the bed for an hour yesterday." text="Yesterday I lay on the bed. (lie down; the past tense is lay)" /> |
| lie | lie – lied – lied | to tell a lie, regular | <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="He lied to me about the money." text="He lied to me. (to tell a lie; regular)" /> |
| lay | lay – laid – laid | to place, transitive | <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="She laid the baby on the bed." text="She laid the baby on the bed. (to place; transitive)" /> |

The trap is that the past tense of "to lie down" happens to be lay: **The book is lying on the desk** (currently lying) is right, **The book is laying on the desk** is wrong.

## Self-check

Run through all 14 groups once; copy the groups you confuse into an error notebook, come back the next day and tap the example words to listen again — it counts as passed only when you can hear the difference.
