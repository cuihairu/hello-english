# Root families

The [Word roots](/roots) page tells the origin story of each root, and the [Roots and affixes](/exam/roots-and-affixes) page breaks words down for exam revision; this page puts the derivatives of one root side by side, so you can see the core meaning reappearing in different words. Three families: spect (to look), duc/duct (to lead), and fer (to bring).

## The spect family: to look

Latin specere "to look". The whole family circles one "looking", with the prefix setting the direction:

| Word | Breakdown | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <SpeakButton word="inspect" text="inspect" /> | in- (inward) + spect | looking inside → to inspect |
| <SpeakButton word="respect" text="respect" /> | re- (again) + spect | keeping re-seeing in the eye → to respect |
| <SpeakButton word="spectator" text="spectator" /> | spect + -ator (one who) | one who looks → a spectator |
| <SpeakButton word="prospect" text="prospect" /> | pro- (forward) + spect | what is seen forward → a prospect |
| <SpeakButton word="suspect" text="suspect" /> | sus- (under) + spect | watching from underneath → to suspect |
| <SpeakButton word="perspective" text="perspective" /> | per- (through) + spect | looking through something → a perspective, perspective |
| <SpeakButton word="spectacle" text="spectacle" /> | spect + -acle | something held up to be seen → a sight, a spectacle (spectacles = glasses) |
| <SpeakButton word="retrospect" text="retrospect" /> | retro- (backward) + spect | looking back → to look back |

The Greek cousin is the scope family: telescope (tēle = far + looking → telescope), microscope (small + looking → microscope). Latin and Greek each left one branch of "looking" — see [Word roots](/roots#spect-to-look-latin-specere).

## The duc/duct family: to lead

Latin ducere "to lead, to guide". The root fixes the "leading" action, the prefix fixes where to:

| Word | Breakdown | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <SpeakButton word="educate" text="educate" /> | e- (out) + duc | drawing potential out → to educate |
| <SpeakButton word="introduce" text="introduce" /> | intro- (inward) + duc | leading in → to introduce |
| <SpeakButton word="produce" text="produce" /> | pro- (forward) + duc | bringing forward → to produce |
| <SpeakButton word="reduce" text="reduce" /> | re- (back) + duc | leading back → to reduce |
| <SpeakButton word="conduct" text="conduct" /> | con- (together) + duct | leading along → to conduct, and by extension "behavior" |
| <SpeakButton word="induce" text="induce" /> | in- (inward) + duc | leading in, persuading → to induce |
| <SpeakButton word="deduce" text="deduce" /> | de- (down from) + duc | inferring downward from the premises → to deduce |
| <SpeakButton word="aqueduct" text="aqueduct" /> | aqua (water) + duct | a channel leading water → an aqueduct |

One test point: induce goes with induction (inductive reasoning, from specific to general) and deduce with deduction (deductive reasoning, from general to specific) — two opposite directions of inference, one prefix each.

## The fer family: to bring

Latin ferre "to bring, to bear". The whole family is "carrying something somewhere", with the prefix fixing the destination:

| Word | Breakdown | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <SpeakButton word="transfer" text="transfer" /> | trans- (across) + fer | carrying across a distance → to transfer, to change trains |
| <SpeakButton word="refer" text="refer" /> | re- (back) + fer | bringing back → to refer to, to consult |
| <SpeakButton word="prefer" text="prefer" /> | pre- (before) + fer | bringing something to the front → to prefer |
| <SpeakButton word="confer" text="confer" /> | con- (together) + fer | bringing together → to confer, to award (confer a degree) |
| <SpeakButton word="suffer" text="suffer" /> | sub- (under) + fer | carrying on underneath → to suffer |
| <SpeakButton word="differ" text="differ" /> | dif- (apart) + fer | carrying to two sides → to differ |
| <SpeakButton word="offer" text="offer" /> | ob- (toward) + fer | handing across in front → to offer |
| <SpeakButton word="infer" text="infer" /> | in- (inward) + fer | drawing a conclusion in from the evidence → to infer |
| <SpeakButton word="fertile" text="fertile" /> | the adjectival branch of fer | able to bring forth fruit → fertile |

fer and the native English word bear are the same PIE word from six thousand years ago (\*bher- "to carry") — see [Word roots · fer](/roots#fer-to-bring-latin-ferre).

## How I memorize root families

I learn the core meaning first (to look, to lead, to bring), then read the direction from the prefix: pro- forward, re- back, in- inward, sub- under, trans- across. When a word refuses to break, do not force it — suffer is already far from "carrying", so learn the common sense first and use the etymology only as an association clue.
