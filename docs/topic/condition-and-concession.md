# Condition and concession

The adverbial clauses on the [grammar page](/grammar) cover structure and position; this record concerns connector choice and common collocation problems for the two kinds of clauses — condition and concession. They are regulars in cloze connector blanks and in translation of conditional sentences, and they are also where direct transfer from Chinese habits breaks down most often.

## The condition family: five gaps besides if

Besides if, each conditional connector has its own temperament:

| Connector | Sense | Tap to hear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| unless | unless (= if…not) | <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="You will fail unless you work harder." text="You will fail unless you work harder. (fail if you do not work harder)" /> |
| as/so long as | as long as | <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="You can go out as long as you are back by ten." text="You can go out as long as you are back by ten." /> |
| provided (that) | provided (that) | <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="Provided that it doesn't rain, we'll go hiking." text="Provided that it doesn't rain, we'll go hiking." /> |
| in case | in case of; just in case | <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="Take an umbrella in case it rains." text="Take an umbrella in case it rains. (preparing beforehand — a different feel from the condition sense of if)" /> |

unless already carries the negation, so do not add not to the sentence: unless you don't work is a double-negative wreck. After in case the simple present stands for the future, not will.

## Imperative + and/or: conditions hidden in the punctuation

"Imperative + and/or + future sentence" is a variant of the conditional, and and means "then", or means "otherwise":

- <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="Study hard, and you will pass the exam." text="Study hard, and you will pass the exam. (= If you study hard, …)" />
- <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="Hurry up, or we will miss the train." text="Hurry up, or we will miss the train. (otherwise we miss it)" />

In cloze, when you see "imperative + blank + future sentence", the blank is almost always and or or.

## The concession family: two iron rules for although

although / though introduce concession, and although does not join with but — the direct transfer of the Chinese "although… but…" is the most frequent error:

- <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="Although it was raining, we went out." text="Although it was raining, we went out. (with although there is no but)" />

though is more flexible: it can act as an adverb at the end of a sentence (It was raining; we went out, though), and it can take part in inversion (adjective fronted); although can only be a conjunction:

- <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="Poor though he was, he never complained." text="Poor though he was, he never complained. (inverted concession — although cannot do this)" />

## even if vs even though: hypothesis and fact, one word apart

even if is hypothesis "even if" (not yet happened); even though is fact "even though" (already happened):

- <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="Even if it rains, we will go." text="Even if it rains, we will go. (even if it does rain, we go — not yet happened)" />
- <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="Even though it rained, we went." text="Even though it rained, we went. (even though it rained — already happened)" />

## despite and in spite of: prepositions do not take a clause

despite / in spite of are prepositions, so they take only a noun or a gerund after them, and **never a clause**; to take a clause, switch to although / the fact that:

- <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="Despite the heavy traffic, we arrived on time." text="Despite the heavy traffic, we arrived on time. (despite + noun)" />
- <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="Despite feeling tired, she kept working." text="Despite feeling tired, she kept working. (despite + gerund)" />

Vocabulary questions love to set traps on the word class of although / despite / in spite of / because of: if a full sentence follows the blank, pick the conjunction; if a noun follows, pick the preposition.

## The three faces of while

while appears at a very high rate in cloze, and its three senses are decided by position:

- when: <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="While I was cooking, the phone rang." text="While I was cooking, the phone rang. (the time background of something in progress)" />
- although (at the start of a sentence): <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="While I understand your point, I can't agree." text="While I understand your point, I can't agree. (concession)" />
- whereas (contrast): <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="I like tea, while my sister prefers coffee." text="I like tea, while my sister prefers coffee. (parallel contrast)" />

## One more frequently tested rule: the future inside a conditional clause does not wear a will costume

Time and conditional clauses use the simple present in place of the future (main future, subordinate present); for the detailed rules, see [Tense comparison](/topic/tense-comparison). Counterfactual conditionals (hypotheses contrary to fact) go a different way: the tense retreats one step, and the rules are on the [grammar page · mood and the subjunctive](/grammar#mood-and-the-subjunctive).
