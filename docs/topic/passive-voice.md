# Passive voice

The [grammar page](/grammar) covers how the passive is built; here I record the things I check when judging whether a sentence suits the passive, and whether a passive construction is correct. It is a guaranteed test point in cloze and translation every year: the translation technique "passive to active" rests on first recognizing the passive in English.

## Two questions to settle it

1. **Is the receiver of the action the better subject?** Receiver of the action is the topic center, and the doer is unknown or unimportant (the by phrase could go either way) → passive.
2. **Is the verb transitive?** Without an object there is nothing to move into the subject slot, and the passive is out of the question.

| Progressive | am/is/are being + done | <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="The road is being repaired." text="The road is being repaired. (being repaired now)" /> |
| Perfect | has/have been + done | <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="The report has been finished." text="The report has been finished. (already finished)" /> |
| Modal | modal + be + done | <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="The work must be done by Friday." text="The work must be done by Friday." /> |

Past simple was/were done and past perfect had been done come off the same assembly line — swap the auxiliary, keep the skeleton.

## These verbs do not go passive

Intransitive verbs have no object (happen, occur, rise); have (to own), hold (to hold), fit, suit, lack are by habit left unpassive; and it also fails when the object is a reflexive or a reciprocal pronoun:

- <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="A car accident happened here last night." text="A car accident happened here last night. (happen is intransitive — no passive)" />
- <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="This jacket fits me well." text="This jacket fits me well. (do not say I am fitted by…)" />

## Phrasal verbs go passive: the preposition is not allowed to be lost

Phrasal verbs like take care of, look into, put off treat the verb and preposition as one piece when they turn passive — the preposition must move along:

- <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="The patients were taken good care of." text="The patients were taken good care of. (only complete when of closes it)" />
- <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="The case is being looked into." text="The case is being looked into. (into is not dropped)" />

Cloze and error-correction love to set a blank on the preposition: when you see a lone preposition stranded at the end of a passive sentence, do not rush to call it wrong.

## The get passive: accidents and experiences

The be passive states a normal condition; the get passive carries a sense of "having met with, an accident", and cannot take a by phrase:

- <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="He got hurt in the match." text="He got hurt in the match. (got hurt — an accident coloring)" />
- <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="She gets paid every Friday." text="She gets paid every Friday. (payday — common in spoken English)" />

## A passive construction or a linking construction: look for action or state

be + past participle reads two ways: action (passive voice) and state (linking construction). gone, finished, interested, excited and the like are already pure adjectives with no corresponding "action passive":

- <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="I am interested in painting." text="I am interested in painting. (state: interested — not the action of 'being made interested')" />
- <SpeakButton kind="sentence" word="All the leaves are gone." text="All the leaves are gone. (state: gone)" />

How to tell: the passive can take a by phrase and can turn progressive; a linking construction can do neither.

## One more frequently tested rule: the "it is said" pattern, and its two transformations

It is said that he is rich (an it-whit clause as formal subject) ↔ He is said to be rich (the person moves to the subject slot, followed by an infinitive). The same pattern covers It is believed / reported / known that… The verb forms of the two transformations must mesh: It is said that he **lives**… ↔ He is said to **live**… (simple present corresponds to to do); It is said that he **was**… ↔ He is said to **have lived**… (past corresponds to to have done).
