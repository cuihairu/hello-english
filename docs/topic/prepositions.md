# Prepositions

Prepositions are regulars in cloze and vocabulary questions, testing two things: whether fixed collocations are memorized, and whether the semantic division is clear. My preposition notes are organized here as "time in three ways → place in three ways → confusable directions → fixed collocations → transitive traps", and the example sentences are tappable.

## Time in three ways: in / on / at

| Preposition | What it covers | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| in | Long spans: years, months, seasons, parts of day | in 2026, in May, in the morning |
| on | A specific day: dates, weekdays, the day of a holiday | on Monday, on May 4th, on Sunday morning |
| at | A time point: clock times, noon, midnight | at six, at noon, at night |

Memory framing: the range goes from large to small — in covers the big spans, on lands on a particular day, at nails down a single point. **For a specific day's morning or evening use on** (on Monday morning); only a general morning or evening takes in (in the morning).

## Place in three ways: in / on / at

| Preposition | What it covers | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| at | A point: a door, a station, a school | at the door, at the bus stop, at school |
| in | Inside and large ranges: a room, a city, a country | in the room, in Beijing, in China |
| on | A surface and a line: a wall, a desktop, a transport route | on the wall, on the desk, on the bus |

The transport framing: bus, train, plane — things you can stand in and walk about in — take on (on the bus); car — things you sit inside — takes in (in the car).

## Confusable directions: over / above, under / below

- **over** directly above, often with a sense of covering or crossing over: a bridge **over** the river.
- **above** higher than, not necessarily directly above: The plane flew **above** the clouds.
- **under** directly below, the counterpart of over: The cat slept **under** the table.
- **below** lower than, the counterpart of above: The valley is 500 meters **below** the peak.

## High-frequency fixed collocations

| Collocation | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| insist on | to insist on doing something | He insisted **on** paying the bill. |
| consist of | to be composed of (no passive) | The committee consists **of** ten members. |
| depend on / rely on | to rely on | You can rely **on** her. |
| account for | to explain; to make up (a share) | imports account **for** 30% of the total |
| accuse sb. of | to accuse someone of | He was accused **of** theft. |
| blame sb. for / blame sth. on | to blame | They blamed the delay **on** the weather. |
| warn sb. of / about | to warn | She warned me **of** the risk. |
| deprive sb. of | to deprive | be deprived **of** the chance |
| rob sb. of | to rob someone of | rob him **of** his wallet |
| charge sb. with | to charge (with a crime) | He was charged **with** murder. |
| provide sb. with / provide sth. for | to provide | They provide us **with** tools. |
| spend … on | to spend | spend two hours **on** the report |
| prefer A to B | to prefer | I prefer tea **to** coffee. |
| be different from | to differ from | Mine is different **from** yours. |

Compare one pair: compare A **with** B is "putting the two side by side", compare A **to** is "likening A to B" (Shakespeare compared the world to a stage).

## Transitive traps: a preposition added or missing

These verbs are already transitive and take an object with no preposition; the questions often set a blank on the superfluous about or with:

| Verb | Correct | Typical error |
| --- | --- | --- |
| discuss | discuss the problem | discuss ~~about~~ the problem |
| marry | marry her; be married to her | marry ~~with~~ her |
| reach | reach Beijing | reach ~~to~~ Beijing |
| answer | answer the question | answer ~~to~~ the question |
| enter | enter the room | enter ~~into~~ the room (enter into is used only with abstract nouns such as agreements and discussions) |
| attend | attend the meeting | attend ~~to~~ the meeting (attend to means "to attend upon", a different sense) |

The noun direction is learned separately: the answer **to** the question, the key **to** the door, the entrance **to** the hall — the "leading to, corresponding to" relation takes to.

## Preposition + relative pronoun

In "preposition + which/whom" structures no that may follow the preposition: the house **in which** I lived is correct, \*\*in that is wrong. Which preposition to choose depends on its collocation with the antecedent (in the house → in which) — see the relative-clause section of the [grammar page](/grammar).
