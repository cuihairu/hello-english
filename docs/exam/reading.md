## 1. The structure of the reading passages

### Section A (4 passages, 20 points)

- About 300–450 words each
- 5 multiple-choice questions each
- Passage types: mainly expository and argumentative

### Section B (1 blog + comments, 5 points)

- 1 blog post + 4–5 reader comments
- 5 multiple-choice questions
- Tests the distinction of viewpoints and the attitude of the comments

## 4. High-frequency question types

### Common question types of Section A

| Question type | How often tested | Typical phrasing |
|------|---------|---------|
| Detail comprehension | Highest | According to the passage, ... |
| Inference | High | It can be inferred that... / What does the author imply? |
| Main idea | High | What is the main idea? / Best title? |
| Word meaning | Medium | The word "X" is closest in meaning to... |
| Viewpoint and attitude | Medium | What is the author's attitude toward...? |
| Paragraph gist | Medium | What is Paragraph X mainly about? |

### Question types specific to Section B (blog comments)

| Question type | How often tested | Typical phrasing |
|------|---------|---------|
| Distinguishing comment viewpoints | Highest | Which comment supports/disagrees with...? |
| Understanding a comment's content | High | Which comment gives a personal reason...? |
| Judging the author's view | High | What is the author's view on...? |
| Inference | Medium | What can be inferred from...? |

## 5. How I handle reading questions

### 1. Main-idea questions

**Method:**
- Pay attention to the first sentence of the first paragraph and the last sentence of the last paragraph
- Find the keywords that recur through the passage
- Rule out options that are too broad or too narrow

**Typical features of wrong options:**
- Over-generalizing: using a detail of one paragraph in place of the whole main idea
- Too broad: going beyond the scope of the passage
- Fabricating: information the passage never mentions

### 2. Detail comprehension questions

**Method:**
- Locate the keyword and return to the original text
- Mind synonymous replacement (the answer is usually not a verbatim sentence from the passage)
- Mind the qualifiers: all, most, some, never, always

### 3. Inference questions

**Method:**
- Infer reasonably from the information in the passage
- Do not over-infer
- The answer must have support in the original text

### 4. Word-meaning questions

**Method:**
- Use the context clues (synonym, antonym, definition, example)
- Mind the logical relation of the sentence the word is in
- Substitute the options and check

### 5. Blog-comment questions (specific to Section B)

**Method:**
- Read the blog body first and grasp the author's view
- Read the comments one by one and mark each one's position
- Distinguish: supports the author / opposes the author / adds to the view / personal experience

## 7. Preparation record

1. **One passage in close reading every day**: choose an English expository or argumentative piece of 300–450 words
2. **Follow current affairs**: technology, environment and psychology articles are the hot spots of the questions
3. **Practice locating**: find quickly where the keywords of a question sit in the original text
4. **A sense of synonymous replacement**: the right answer is almost always a synonymous expression of the original
5. **Special training on blog comments**: Section B has been a fixed question type in recent years and needs dedicated practice
6. **Timed training**: five passages (including the blog) in 45 minutes, about 8–9 minutes each
