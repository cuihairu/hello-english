# Exam timing

> Organized from the time markings on the real papers of 2013–2025: Paper 1 is 100 minutes and Paper 2 is 50 minutes, 150 minutes and 100 points for the whole paper (the 2024 paper marks 15/10/45/10/20 minutes and point values part by part; the structure has been consistent across the years). The question types and high-frequency test points of each part are in [High-frequency test-point analysis](/exam/key-points).

## 2. Baseline pace and checkpoints

Follow the time allotted on the paper and control the pace with six checkpoints:

| Checkpoint (after the start) | Should be finished | Signal |
|----------------|--------|------|
| 15 minutes | Spoken communication | Move on to vocabulary |
| 25 minutes | Vocabulary | Move on to reading |
| 70 minutes | Reading comprehension | Move on to cloze |
| 80 minutes | Cloze | Move on to short-text completion |
| 100 minutes | Paper 1 | Hand in Paper 1, collect Paper 2 |
| 120 minutes | Translation | Move on to writing |

## 3. Internal pace of each part

- **Spoken communication**: 1.5 minutes per Section A item; the Section B long dialogue with 4 blanks takes about 6 minutes — fix the speaker and the position in the flow first, then choose the sentence
- **Vocabulary**: 1 minute per item; if an item passes 1.5 minutes, mark it and skip, then fill it back in at the end
- **Reading comprehension**: about 9 minutes per passage (5 items each in Section A); the Section B blog comments also 9 minutes
- **Cloze**: 1 minute per blank; the first paragraph has no blanks, so read through to set the tone before putting pen to paper
- **Short-text completion**: about 6.5 minutes per passage — the first step of filling words inside the phrase takes 3 minutes (part of speech at the door, one word used once), the second step back into the text takes 3.5 minutes (locate by the semantic hook)
- **Translation**: read through 2 minutes → write 15 minutes → reread and check 3 minutes
- **Writing**: read the task and outline 5 minutes → write 20 minutes → check 5 minutes

## 4. Points-to-time value

| Question type | Points | Time | Score efficiency (points/min) | Judgment |
|------|------|------|------------------|------|
| Short-text completion | 20 | 20 | 1.00 | Highest — protect the accuracy rate |
| Vocabulary | 10 | 10 | 1.00 | High — the rhythm zone for free points |
| Cloze | 10 | 10 | 1.00 | High — the rhythm zone for free points |
| Spoken communication | 10 | 15 | 0.67 | Medium |
| Reading comprehension | 25 | 45 | 0.56 | Medium-low — speed comes from training |
| Translation | 10 | 20 | 0.50 | Low — protect the structure points |
| Writing | 15 | 30 | 0.50 | Low — protect structural completeness |

**What this implies strategically**: when time is tight, protect the accuracy rate of the 1-point-per-minute zone (short-text completion, vocabulary, cloze) first; reading, translation and writing have low score efficiency, so speed comes from practice beforehand and you should not fight for speed in the exam room.

## 5. Going over time and trade-offs

1. **One item over by 1.5×** → mark it and skip, fill it back in at the end, do not linger
2. **One reading passage over 12 minutes** → do the sure ones first, in the order "details → main idea → inference"
3. **Not enough time for writing** → a complete 3-paragraph structure comes before fine wording; near the lower word limit, finish it first and polish later
4. **Filling in the answer sheet** — fill as you go to avoid mistakes from filling it all at once; before handing in, leave time to check for the signals (missed items, misalignment)

## 6. Recording mock practice

1. One complete 150-minute mock per week (paper assembly and grading in [Mock paper records and review](/exam/mock-exam-guide))
2. Self-assess against the six checkpoints, record the actual time spent on each part, and compare against the baseline to find the drift
3. Train hard on reading speed (9 minutes per passage meets the standard) and outline speed in writing (an outline in 5 minutes)

> **A note on the markings**: the times and point values follow the markings on the real papers; the checkpoints, internal pace and trade-off strategies are preparation suggestions derived by this site from the paper data, and are **not** an official exam guide. For the solving strategy of each question type, see the corresponding pages of the [High-frequency test-point analysis](/exam/key-points) series.
