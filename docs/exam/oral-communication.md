## 1. Question-type structure (after the 2013 reform)

| Section | Format | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Section A | Two everyday dialogues, each with 3 blanks; the A/B/C options are original sentences "taken away" from the dialogue, to be chosen and put back | 6 of the 10 points |
| Section B | One long dialogue (a celebrity interview in the early years, and more recently a transactional long dialogue as well), with 4 blanks; four options A/B/C/D | 4 of the 10 points |

The key insight: the options are sentences taken away from the original text, not synonymous rewrites. What you are doing is not "translating the option", but finding for each sentence its original place in the flow of the dialogue.

Three regularities:

1. **Section A is life drama**: getting things done, seeing a doctor, at home, among classmates — the scenes are extremely everyday, the vocabulary is not hard, and what is tested is "how to take up the conversation".
2. **Section B is changing faces**: six straight years of celebrity interviews from 2013–2018; from 2019 it turned to transactional and job-hunting long dialogues (course registration, interviews, appointments, lost-property reports), with only 2025 returning to chatty conversation — an interview is not there every year.
3. **Getting things done and service is the first big category**: nine dialogues cover parking, library cards, the post office, air tickets, course registration, broadband, customer service, lost property, and clinic registration. The flow chain of "need → check the details → give options → confirm → close" is the constant behind all the changes.

## 4. Blank-setting regularities

Five types of blank patterns, each with a fixed "paired-sentence hook":

1. **A yes/no question is the blank → the next sentence opens with Yes/No.** Is this your first visit? → Yes, it is. (2024 D3); I thought you said there were three men → No, there were two men and a girl. (2014 D1)
2. **A special question is the blank → the next sentence gives the information directly.** What is your name? → My name is Karen Huang. (2019 D3); How much do I owe you? → Just $13. (2013 D2)
3. **An emotion or evaluation is the blank → the next sentence empathizes or presses further.** I was really nervous about it. → I can understand that. (2024 D1)
4. **A request or proposal is the blank → the next sentence accepts, politely declines, or offers an alternative.** May I have your driver's license, please? → Here it is. (2015 D2); Do you happen to have an opening in the morning? → How about Tuesday at 8:00 am? (2024 D3, rescheduling)
5. **An advancing addition is the blank → the sentences before and after share the same topic of the same turn.** The three blanks in the parking explanation of 2015 D1 all land on nodes of the clerk's explaining chain.

## 5. How I handle the questions

1. **Fix which sentence the blank belongs to**: if the blank is in the later sentence, look at what the earlier one asks or wants; if the blank is in the earlier sentence, look at what the later one answers — Yes/No works back to a yes/no question, specific information works back to a special question.
2. **Identify the speakers**: the customer asks and the server answers; the interviewer asks and the interviewee answers. Read the option for its tone and information load, and do not put the interviewee's answer on the interviewer.
3. **Catch reference and repetition**: it / that / them point back to the interview, the purse, the books; a noun repeated inside the option is the hook.
4. **Follow the flow chain when getting things done**: a transactional dialogue advances "need → check the details → choose → confirm"; when you get stuck, use the position in the flow to narrow the options.
5. **Reread the whole text**: after filling in, read through once and check whether the chain of logic and the chain of politeness (greeting → thanks → farewell) are broken.

## 7. Preparation record

- 10 minutes for 10 points, one of the question types with the best return on investment: all the answers are hidden inside the dialogue and no outside knowledge is needed.
- Turn the two hook pairs "yes/no question → Yes/No" and "special question → specific information" into reflexes, and the six points of Section A are secured first.
- Use the three dialogues of each year's paper as speaking material for reading along: the scene sentences are both the key to the answers and a ready-made reserve for writing and speaking.
