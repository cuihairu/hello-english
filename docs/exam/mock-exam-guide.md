# Mock paper records and review

> Organized from the 16 assembled papers of 2010–2025 and the pacing baseline of [Exam timing](/exam/timing), for assembling papers, grading and reviewing them yourself. No new questions are written here; all the material comes from the past-paper pages on this site. For the source and the reliability of the answers of each paper, see the page head of each past-paper page and [Past-paper sources](/exam/papers/sources).

## 1. Principles of assembling a paper

1. **Full authenticity first**: a full mock is always assembled from real papers, never by mixing your own questions
2. **Completeness decides the use**: only years with complete question texts and answers serve as full mocks; a paper with gaps is downgraded to sectional practice (see the table below)
3. **Use the years you have not yet read closely first** as full mocks; the ones you have read closely go to sectional timed training
4. **Hold the newest papers back as the finale**: keep 2023 and 2025 for full-authentic mocks in the last two weeks

**Grading standards (suggested by this site)**:

- Objective questions (spoken communication, vocabulary, reading, cloze, short-text completion) are graded against the reference answers of each past-paper page: the pages of 2010—2012, 2014—2017, 2020, 2022 and 2024 have a "reference answers" quick-lookup section at the end; the pages of 2013, 2018, 2019, 2021, 2023 and 2025 mark the answers alongside the questions (in grouped rows or bold rows under the item), with no quick-lookup section at the end. The answers of 2013 short-text completion (56–75) were derived from the phrase-completion relation and are **to be verified**, and like 2015 and 2024 belong to self-assessment for reference only
- Translation is self-assessed against the reference translation by points: weigh semantic accuracy and fluent wording alike, and mark omissions and mistranslations one by one; **the Paper 2 papers of 2015, 2018, 2021 and 2023 have no reference translation** (not attached to the source, and marked **[to be supplied]** on the past-paper pages), so self-assess against the rendering points and scoring points in [High-frequency grammar and translation](/exam/grammar-and-translation); the 2012 Section B and the 2016 English original are this site's trial/back translation (**to be proofread**), and the 2024 translation has watermark-obscured places proposed for completion, so mind this when comparing
- Writing is self-assessed against the model essay: does it cover the points of the task, is the language accurate, is the structure complete; **for years with no model essay (2015, 2018, 2021, 2023, 2024)** self-assess against the framework and sentence patterns in [High-frequency writing](/exam/writing)

## 4. The review flow

1. Grade and fill in the grading sheet
2. Attribute each wrong item to the matching analysis page: wrong vocabulary → [High-frequency vocabulary](/exam/vocabulary); wrong reading → [High-frequency reading](/exam/reading); wrong spoken communication → [High-frequency spoken communication](/exam/oral-communication); wrong cloze and short-text completion → [High-frequency cloze and short-text completion](/exam/cloze-and-completion); weak translation and writing → [High-frequency grammar and translation](/exam/grammar-and-translation) and [High-frequency writing](/exam/writing)
3. Find the two or three parts that lost the most points, train them specifically next week, then mock again
4. Check the time spent against the six checkpoints of [Exam timing](/exam/timing), and review any overrun part separately

## 5. Suggested frequency

- **Middle of preparation**: one paper every two weeks
- **One month before the exam**: one paper a week
- **The last two weeks**: use 2023 and 2025 as the final two full-authentic mocks, executed strictly to the 150 minutes and the six checkpoints

> **A note on the markings**: the standards of paper assembly, grading and review are preparation suggestions organized by this site from the past papers on the site, and are **not** an official exam guide; the reliability of the answers differs from paper to paper, and for details see the markings at the page head of each past-paper page.
