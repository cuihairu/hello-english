# High-frequency test-point analysis

> Analysis based on the real papers of 2013–2025, for preparing the English exam of the equivalent-academic-level master's degree route. The 2026 prediction for each question type is in [Key-point prediction](/exam/2026-prediction).

## Core findings

### 1. Vocabulary: synonym replacement is the absolute core

All 10 items of Part II Vocabulary every year test **synonym-replacement ability**. Among the 148 vocabulary items verified on this site (including same-type Section A items from the pre-2013 papers), word items account for about 73% and phrase / fixed-collocation items for about 27%; inside the word items nouns (33 items) and verbs (31 items) dominate, with 24 adjectives and 14 adverbs.

### 2. Translation: no strict topic rotation, academic topics come in runs

The translation topics of 13 years show no strict rotation: in the early years philosophical and social topics alternated as the base, 2020–2022 ran three years of academic topics, technology appeared for the first time in 2024, and 2025 returned to environment. Sentence patterns are dominated by relative clauses, the passive, and non-finite verbs.

### 3. Writing: the argumentative structure is fixed

Topics are mainly opinion-essay type, and a 4-paragraph structure (introduction — argument 1 — argument 2 — conclusion) is the safest. Topics lean toward environment, education, and technology ethics.

### 4. Reading: technology and psychology are the hot spots

Passages on technological development (AI, space, driverless cars) rank among the most frequent (about 15%); psychology is the single largest topic (about 20%); environment and ecology appear steadily. The blog-comment type of Section B has been a fixed test point in recent years.

### 5. Spoken communication: Section B is changing faces, and the answers are all inside the dialogue

2013–2018 ran six straight years of celebrity interviews in Section B; the verifiable Section B of 2019–2024 are all transactional long dialogues (course registration, job interviews, clinic appointments); 2025 returned to chatty conversation and celebrity interviews did not reappear. Section A is life drama: getting things done, seeing a doctor, at home, among classmates — the answers do not rely on outside knowledge, they rely on the pairing hook of "answer whatever is asked". Details in [High-frequency spoken communication](/exam/oral-communication).

### 6. Cloze and short-text completion: the "a study finds" frame is the shared base

Cloze runs narrative plus social-science expository writing (children and society, technology and environment); short-text completion runs short popular science, social science and daily life; both passage skeletons cannot escape the "a study finds…" "research finds" frame. Of the 20 points of short-text completion, the first step of phrase filling is a pure part-of-speech-plus-meaning exercise, and is the easiest part of the 30-point block to gain quickly. Details in [High-frequency cloze and short-text completion](/exam/cloze-and-completion).

### 7. Suggested time allocation for preparation

- **Early stage (60%)**: vocabulary + reading (lay the foundation)
- **Middle stage (25%)**: translation + writing (practice output)
- **Late stage (15%)**: spoken communication + cloze + short-text completion (overall improvement)
