## 2. High-frequency vocabulary statistics

### 1. Statistics by part of speech

The sample is 148 items (2010–2012 counting only Section A), of which:

| Type | Count | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Word-meaning discrimination | 108 | 73% |
| Phrase / fixed-collocation discrimination | 40 | 27% |

Among the 108 word items, nouns are most numerous (33 items), then verbs (31 items), 24 adjectives and 14 adverbs; plus 3 items of compound part of speech (slump, authorized, commuting), 2 prepositions (beyond, beneath), and 1 conjunction (lest):

| Category | Representative words | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verbs | suspend, assess, surpass, consolidate, convert | Often the synonymous replacement of common verbs |
| Nouns | trait, liability, scrutiny, consensus, deficiency | Often abstract and academic nouns |
| Adjectives / adverbs | fragile, deliberate, rigorous, sufficient, deliberately | Often judgments of tone and degree |

The words or synonymous relations below appear twice or more in the verified years:

| Word / relation | Occurrences | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |

| The enough class of meaning | sufficient → enough; ample → enough | High frequency of the sufficient-amount class |
| The deliberate family | 2010, 2011, 2013, 2017 | "Intentional" was tested four times: purposefully → deliberately, on purpose → deliberately, deliberate → intentional (2011 and 2017 of the same type) |
| surpass → exceed | 2010, 2016; transcend → exceed (2022) | "To exceed" is high frequency, with exceed as the correct option three times |
| comply with → observe | 2012, 2018 | The same phrase replaced of the same type twice; observe was also an underlined word in 2010 (observe → follow) |

| perspective | 2021, 2022 | An underlined word in 2021 (→ viewpoint) and the correct option in 2022 (lens → perspective) |

| The "approximately" chain | 2011, 2012 | in the neighborhood of → approximately; approximately → roughly |
| "Hardly any" | 2011, 2016 | scarcely → hardly; barely → hardly |

| The explain/show class of meaning | demonstrated → showed; elaborated → explained; put across → explain; interpretation → explanation | The "explain / show" class is high frequency |
| The control/cause class of meaning | at the mercy of → under the control of; attributed to → caused by | Causal and dominative relations are often tested |

### 2. The "regulated usages" that need special attention

These are not simply memorized Chinese meanings, but must be memorized together with their collocations and context:

| Expression | Regulated usage | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| eligible for | be eligible for sth. | means "qualified to obtain / apply for" |
| attribute A to B | attribute A to B | means "to attribute A to B" |
| appeal to sb. | sth. appeal to sb. | means "something attracts someone" |
| be authorized to do | be authorized to do sth. | means "authorized to do something" |
| catch up with sth. | catch up with sth. | means "to catch up with the pace / understand what has been done" |
| carry out a plan / task | carry out sth. | means "to carry out a plan, task, investigation" |
| call off a class / meeting | call off sth. | means "to cancel an event" |
| give out information | give out sth. | in the papers this tests "to leak information", not "to hand out papers" |
| in excess of | in excess of sth. | means "to exceed …" |
| leaf through a book | leaf through sth. | means "to skim through quickly" |
| be inclined to | be inclined to do / toward sth. | means "to be inclined to" |
| in terms of | in terms of sth. | means "in terms of; with regard to" |
| put across an idea / point | put across sth. | means "to express an idea or point clearly" |
| consist in | consist in sth. / doing sth. | means "to lie in", do not confuse it with consist of |
| wear off | pain / effect wears off | means "for an effect to fade away gradually" |
| comply with | comply with rules / decisions | means "to comply with rules and rulings", tested twice, 2012 and 2018 |
| get down to sth. | get down to (doing) sth. | to is a preposition; means "to get down to serious business" |
| make way for | make way for sth. | means "to make way for; to clear space for" |
| call for | call for sth. | means "to need, to require", equal to require |
| lean on sb. | lean on sb. = count on sb. | means "to rely on someone" |
| cling to sth. | cling to a belief / view | means "to hold fast to a belief or view" |
| under no circumstances | partial inversion at the start of a sentence: Under no circumstances should we… | means "never"; the word order is the test point |

### 3. High-frequency easily-confused phrases

| Expression in the papers | Easily-confused expression | The difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| consist in | consist of | the former means "to lie in", the latter "to be composed of" |
| give out | give off | the former often means "to hand out / to leak / to be used up", the latter "to give off (a smell, heat, light)" |
| call off | put off | the former "to cancel", the latter "to postpone" |
| carry out | work out | the former "to carry out", the latter "to work out / to solve / to exercise" |
| catch up with | keep up with | the former leans to "catch up with the pace / content", the latter to "stay in step" |
| in excess of | in need of | the former means "to exceed", the latter "to need" |
| turn out | make out | the former can mean "to produce / the result is", the latter more often "to make out / to understand" |
| step down | slow down | the former "to step down / to resign", the latter "to slow down" |
| make up | make up for | the former was tested in the papers as "to compose" (→ compose) and "to invent" (→ invent), the latter is "to make up for" |
| as opposed to | in terms of | the former "as opposed to" (2010), the latter "in terms of" (2017) — both prepositional phrases but different in meaning |
| under no circumstances | for no reason | the former "never" (inversion at the start of a sentence), the latter "for no reason"; in 2021 they were each other's distractors |
| take after | take on | the former "to look like / to be like in character", the latter "to take on / to hire / to present" |

## 4. Observations on the data and preparation record

### 1. Vocabulary questions are not simply a matter of memorizing words

The core of the papers is synonymous replacement, but the way the questions are set leans more toward:

- common words replaced by synonymous expressions
- phrases replaced by fixed collocations
- the abstract sense rather than the literal sense

### 2. The share of phrase questions is stably high

In the currently verified sample, phrase / fixed-collocation items make up 27%. This means review cannot only be word lists; it must also cover:

- verb + preposition / adverb
- the common objects of fixed collocations
- the semantic boundaries between easily-confused phrases

### 3. Reference order of investment

It is suggested to review in this order:

1. stop / cancel / change verbs and phrases
2. cause and effect / attribution / explanation expressions
3. qualification, leaning and attitude adjectives and fixed structures
4. the phrase collocations that recur across the papers
