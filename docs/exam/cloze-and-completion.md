# High-frequency cloze and short-text completion

> Organized from the papers on this site that can currently be verified for Part IV Cloze (10 points) and Part V Text Completion (20 points). The two together are 30 points, the largest block of points on the paper.
>
> Years included in the passage statistics: 2013–2025, 13 years in total; the Part IV Cloze and Part V Text Completion passages are complete for both; the cloze of the pre-2013 papers is mostly missing and is not merged into the statistics.
>
> Mock-paper arrangements and the record of error review are in [Mock paper records and review](/exam/mock-exam-guide).

## 1. Question-type structure

| Question type | Format | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Part IV Cloze | One passage of about 250 words with 10 blanks, each blank four options | 10 points |
| Part V Text Completion | Three passages; for each, first pick words from the box to complete a phrase (one blank inside the phrase), then put the completed phrase back into the text | 20 points |

The "two-step blank filling" of short-text completion is the essential feature that sets it apart from cloze: fix the word inside the phrase first, then carry the phrase back to the text to locate it. The first step is a pure part-of-speech-plus-meaning exercise, and only the second step is a context exercise.

## 3. Subject-matter regularities

1. **Cloze is narrative plus social-science expository writing**: one thread runs people and society (the charity girl, gift-giving psychology, negative news, division of dishwashing, homework, Carnegie), one thread runs technology and environment (transport history, population and food, cloud computing, the English countryside, London's flood defenses, artificial intelligence). Narrative in the first paragraph, and the later paragraphs bring in research or unfold an argument — that is the skeleton of the whole piece.
2. **Short-text completion runs three roads — short popular science, social science and daily life**: psychology and health (panic attacks, impulsive spending, positive thinking, sleep and immunity, friendship and happiness, children's number concepts); environment and animals (the decline in insects, the Asian elephant, Earth Hour, ecological balance, the invasion of parrots); technology and cognition (children and screens, the banana with an edible skin, penguin biomimicry, micro-robots, driverless cars, AI predicting fires); social culture (weddings, women at work, library service, the high-heel dispute, Beethoven, tennis, linguistics).
3. **The shared base is the "research finds" frame**: the a study / a report / a survey finds… frame appears almost every year — "a certain study looked at X, the results showed Y, experts think Z" (as in the 2014 cloze a study finds, the 2018 cloze a recent report, the 2025 cloze a McKinsey survey illustrates). Once you recognize this skeleton, you can predict the direction of most paragraphs in ten cases out of nine.
4. **Subject distribution**: the 13 years in the statistics give 52 passages (13 cloze + 39 short-text completion); split roughly by the main field of a passage: about 16 social and humanities, about 11 technology, about 11 psychology and health, about 10 environment and animals, and about 4 language, education and others.

## 4. Blank-setting and option regularities

### The high-frequency blank types of Part IV cloze

1. **Connector and transition blanks**: however, so, though, in other words — fix the logical direction of the context first, then choose the word.
2. **Word-meaning discrimination blanks**: debated / praised / ignored / perceived (2023-47) — four words of the same semantic field weighed against each other.
3. **Grammatical-form blanks**: leading California to ban… (2023-49, non-finite) — participle, tense, active and passive all land in the blank.
4. **Fixed-collocation blanks**: in favor of (2023-50), depend on, keep up with — prepositions and verb phrases are the free-point blanks.

### The two steps of Part V short-text completion

1. **Step one (fill the word inside the phrase)**: look at the part of speech at the blank — a verb slot takes a verb (feel, include, fill), a noun slot takes a noun (variety, danger, question). Each word in the box is used exactly once, so you can work back by elimination.
2. **Step two (carry the phrase back into the text)**: the text leaves semantic hooks before and after the blank — a matching logical subject, clues of number and time (20%, up to a year), cause and contrast with adjacent sentences. Read the hook first, then drop the phrase in.

## 5. The solving method I organize

**Four steps for cloze**:

1. The first sentence has no blank and is the plumb bob of the whole text — understand the first paragraph before putting pen to paper.
2. Catch repetition and collocation: the answer often repeats a word from earlier, or forms a fixed collocation with the preceding word.
3. For a connector blank, fix the logical direction first (contrast or continuation), then pick the word from the options.
4. After all ten blanks are filled, read through once, and review first at the places where the meaning or the logic breaks.

**Three steps for short-text completion**:

1. Do all of the phrase-internal blanks first (step one), entering by part of speech, using up the box.
2. Carry the completed phrases through the text to find their landing spots, advancing passage by passage, setting aside the uncertain ones for now.
3. Reread the three passages and check that each phrase sits firmly in the text both grammatically and semantically.

## 7. My preparation record

- The largest 30-point block, and the highest priority: for cloze practice "fix the plumb bob from the first sentence + the logical direction", and for short-text completion practice "enter by part of speech + the two steps".
- Prepare the topic words by semantic field: the four roads of social and humanities, technology, psychology and health, and environment and animals are the main body of the passages (together about nine-tenths of them); for the vocabulary inside the paper, see the semantic-field list in [High-frequency vocabulary](/exam/vocabulary).
- The grammar blanks in cloze (non-finite verbs, clause connectors) match directly against the sentence-pattern list in [High-frequency grammar and translation](/exam/grammar-and-translation) — patch exactly what went wrong.
- Merge the accumulation of phrase and collocation questions with the fixed-collocation quick-recall in [Vocabulary quick-recall](/exam/vocabulary-memorization) — one effort, benefit in two places.
