# 2026 key-point prediction for the equivalent-qualification English exam

> Based on data analysis of 13 years of real papers, 2013–2025, combined with the trends and regularities of the setting. For vocabulary, the same-type items of the 2010–2012 old papers are counted as well (a sample of 148 items in all). The prediction is for reference only; the actual exam prevails.

## 1. Vocabulary (Part II) prediction

- 10 items a year, all of the **synonymous replacement** type
- Across the 148-item sample of 2010–2023 and 2025: 108 word items (73%) and 40 phrase / fixed-collocation items (27%); among the word items nouns (33 items, about 22%) and verbs (31 items, about 21%) dominate, with 24 adjectives (about 16%) and 14 adverbs (about 9%)
- Recent trend: **familiar words in unfamiliar senses** are on the rise (as coat → cover and inclined → leaning in 2023)

### High-probability repeated replacement pairs

The 148-item sample shows clear evidence of "repeated replacement pairs": the same replacement relation has been re-tested in real papers at intervals of 6–9 years (eligible → qualified from 2016 to 2025, an interval of 9 years; deliberate → intentional from 2011 to 2017; comply with → observe from 2012 to 2018, both an interval of 6 years). Accordingly, the replacement pairs below are worth consolidating first:

| Replacement pair | Evidence of recurrence | Judgment for 2026 |
|--------|---------|----------|
| eligible → qualified | Tested twice, 2016 and 2025 | Just recurred; the "qualification" meaning is still a high-frequency pool |
| deliberate → intentional | Tested twice, 2011 and 2017 | 9 years since the last time, inside the recurrence window |
| comply with → observe | Tested twice, 2012 and 2018 | 8 years since the last time, inside the recurrence window |
| scarcely/barely → hardly | 2011 and 2016 | The "hardly any" meaning is set to be tested |
| surpass/transcend → exceed | 2010, 2016, 2022 | exceed as the correct option three times |

> The complete recurrence list and the semantic-field classification are in section 2, "Already-repeated high-frequency words", of [High-frequency vocabulary](/exam/vocabulary).

### Priority semantic fields for 2026

| Semantic field | Reason for priority |
|--------|---------|
| Stop / cancel | cease, suspend and call off tested repeatedly; called off → canceled again in 2023 |
| Qualification / sufficiency | eligible → qualified recurred; sufficient → enough and ample → enough both tested |
| Explain / illustrate | demonstrated, elaborated, put across → explain appear concentrated |
| Attitude / leaning | deliberate, inclined, reluctant recur across each other |

### The three-step answering method

1. **Judge the part of speech first**: the correct option must share the part of speech with the underlined word, so options of the wrong part of speech can be ruled out first (the part-of-speech distribution of the word-item answers in the sample: 33 nouns, 31 verbs, 24 adjectives, 14 adverbs)
2. **Then distinguish the register**: a written underlined word pairs with a written correct option (affirms → confirms), and a phrase underlined word often pairs with a colloquial simple word (put into practice → apply)
3. **Memorize the phrase as a whole**: the 27% of phrase items cannot be translated word by word and must be memorized in groups (consists in → lies in, lean on → count on)

### Predicted writing topics for 2026

#### Prediction one: artificial intelligence and employment (highest probability)

**Predicted title**:
> **Will Artificial Intelligence Take Away Our Jobs?**
>
> With the rapid development of artificial intelligence, many jobs that used to be done by humans are now being performed by machines. Some people worry that AI will lead to mass unemployment, while others believe it will create new opportunities. What is your opinion?

**Points to cover**:
1. AI does indeed replace some repetitive work
2. But it also creates new fields of employment

### A universal writing template (fitted to the predicted topics)

```
第一段（引言，约 40 词）：
In recent years, [话题] has aroused wide concern among the public.
Some people believe that [观点A], while others argue that [观点B].
As far as I am concerned, I firmly support the view that [我的观点].

第二段（论点一，约 50 词）：
First and foremost, [论点一]. [展开解释].
For example, [举例说明]. This clearly demonstrates that [小结].

第三段（论点二，约 50 词）：
Furthermore, [论点二]. [展开解释].
According to [引用/数据], [支撑证据].
Therefore, [小结].

第四段（结论，约 30 词）：
In conclusion, [重申观点].
Only by [做法] can we [期望结果].
I am convinced that [展望未来].
```

### A pros-and-cons template (simulated)

The second largest type besides the opinion-essay (2020 online learning and 2022 self-employment are both of this type), with the structure "both sides stated + one's own leaning":

```
第一段（引出话题，约 40 词）：
Nowadays, [现象] is becoming increasingly popular.
Like a double-edged sword, it brings both benefits and problems.

第二段（优点，约 45 词）：
On the one hand, [现象] has obvious advantages.
First, [优点一]. For example, [例子].
Moreover, [优点二].

第三段（缺点，约 45 词）：
On the other hand, we cannot ignore its disadvantages.
To begin with, [缺点一]. Besides, [缺点二].

第四段（结论，约 30 词）：
From my perspective, every coin has two sides.
We should [做法/建议] so as to [目标], while avoiding [风险].
```

### Handles for 2026 preparation

**Five blank types — recognize the hook first** (see section 4 of [High-frequency spoken communication](/exam/oral-communication)):

1. **A yes/no question is the blank** → the next sentence opens with Yes/No
2. **A special question is the blank** → the next sentence gives the information directly
3. **An emotion or evaluation is the blank** → the next sentence empathizes or presses further
4. **A request or proposal is the blank** → the next sentence accepts, politely declines, or offers an alternative
5. **An advancing addition is the blank** → the sentences before and after share the same topic of the same turn

**Priority functional sentences**: May I help you? (opening), Could I have… please? (request), That sounds great. (echo), Thank you for… (closing)

## 6. Prediction for cloze and short-text completion

> The complete table of past-year passage topics is in [High-frequency cloze and short-text completion](/exam/cloze-and-completion).

### Cloze (Part IV)

**Recently tested**: 2023 the debate over the pros and cons of homework, 2024 islands made by volcanoes (Bermuda), 2025 the applications, risks and biases of artificial intelligence.

**Predicted topics for 2026**: after technology has been tested in a run, a rotation is possible; social phenomena (urbanization, the impact of the media) or a biography are all possible.

### Short-text completion (Part V)

**The type is fixed**: 3 passages, each with a phrase-completion step and a fill-in-the-blanks step.

**High-frequency topics**:
- Technology applications (2025: driverless cars, AI predicting forest fires)
- Social research (2023: the sharp decline in insects, what sociology is)
- Life and health (2020: sleep and immunity, friendship and happiness)

**The prediction for 2026**: possible topics include AI applications, distance education and healthy living.

### Handles for 2026 preparation

**The four high-frequency cloze blank types** (recognize the blank type before putting pen to paper):

1. **Connector and transition blanks** (however, so, though) — fix the logical direction of the context first, then choose the word
2. **Word-meaning discrimination blanks** — four words of the same semantic field weighed against each other
3. **Grammatical-form blanks** — participle, tense, active and passive all land in the blank
4. **Fixed-collocation blanks** (in favor of, depend on) — prepositions and verb phrases are the free-point blanks

**Run through the free-point collocations first**: in favor of, compared to/with, keep up with, depend on, a variety of, lead to, rather than, rely on (all tested in real papers)

**The two steps of short-text completion**: in the first step fill the word inside the phrase by part of speech (each word in the box used once, so work back by elimination); in the second step carry the completed phrase back to the text to find the semantic hook (a matching logical subject, clues of number and time, cause and contrast between adjacent sentences).

## 7. My preparation-plan record

### The sprint stage (one month before the exam)

1. **Vocabulary**: review the 5 repeated replacement pairs and the 4 priority semantic fields, paired with the "three-step answering method"
2. **Translation**: 2 passages on education using the simulated originals in this page, 2 each on economics and technology ethics (of comparable length to the simulated originals)
3. **Writing**: 1 essay each on AI and employment, and on social media; use the universal template for the opinion-essay and the pros-and-cons template for the pros-and-cons analysis
4. **Reading**: close-read English articles on AI ethics and mental health; train specifically on distinguishing comment attitudes in Section B
5. **Cloze and short-text completion**: run through the 8 free-point collocations and the 4 high-frequency blank types
6. **Mock**: complete 2–3 full mock papers against the six checkpoints of [Exam timing](/exam/timing) (paper assembly and grading in [Mock paper records and review](/exam/mock-exam-guide))
